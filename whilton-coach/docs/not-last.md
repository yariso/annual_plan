# Not last at Karun's Karting Carnival

The plan for four novices in Whilton Mill's hire karts on 4 October 2026, built from a research pass on 19 and 20 September 2026 across the event's own pages, UK karting coaches and venues, and the motor learning literature (Consensus and PubMed). Every figure names its source. The independent verification pass that was meant to check each load-bearing claim ran out of session budget before it finished, so a claim marked "unverified" below rests on the researcher's reading of one or two sources and should be confirmed at the briefing or by a quick search before it is relied on.

Plain rule for reading this: the event facts come from the organiser's site and the venue; the driving advice is what UK coaches write, consistently, and none of it has been trialled on this team; the science is real but its effect sizes are small, so it shapes how the app talks, not what we promise.

## 1. The event, and what last looks like

- Karun Chandhok's charity team race at Whilton Mill, run there since 2011 for the Vishnu Devananda Charitable Trust; the 2026 page calls it the fifteenth consecutive year (source: karunskartingcarnival.mystrikingly.com).
- Sunday 4 October 2026 from 10am, aiming to finish by 4pm (same source).
- 2026 format from the event site: one 30-minute practice and qualifying session, two 60-minute heats, and a 45-minute final for the top twelve teams from each heat (same source). One researcher found an older, undated excerpt describing a 30-minute session then a single 75-minute enduro; treat the heats-and-final format as current and confirm it at the briefing.
- Teams of three or four drivers, and every team makes the same three pit stops, "so it will equalise regardless of the number of drivers" (same source). Minimum age 16, no professional drivers, £320 per team.
- Field: an X post from Karun for a 4 October edition said over 40 teams had signed up; a 2021 team's video description lists P16 in qualifying, 11th in its heat, P23 on the final grid and P15 at the flag, so the final grid was at least 23 that year (unverified beyond those excerpts).
- The karts: Whilton replaced its Sodi RT8 fleet with BirelART N35 390cc karts in September 2024 (source: whiltonmill.co.uk). The N35 has an anti-gas-brake system that cuts the throttle when the brake is pressed (Birel ART rental page; also the CUAC October 2024 report already in docs/validation.md). One pedal or the other, never both.
- Pace: a quick hire driver did 1:04.537 on the International with the chicane on 18 February 2025 (Alpha Timing, Whilton corporate results). Customer reviews report karts up to 3 seconds a lap apart (Tripadvisor, single accounts). No Carnival results were found on Alpha Timing under any of about twenty searches, so the gap between last and mid-field is unknown until the team supplies last year's printout or records the day.
- Layout: not stated for the Carnival. Corporate events run the International, the rental leaderboard is International, and the venue's race club posted a hire-kart chicane clip, so expect the International with the chicane, and confirm at the briefing (unverified).
- Stint maths: four drivers and three stops in a 60-minute heat is four stints of about 14 minutes; in the final about 11. Whether karts are swapped at the stops, the minimum stationary time, and whether the engine stays running were not found: ask.

## 2. The ten changes worth the most, in the coaches' order

Sources agree far more than they differ. A 390cc hire kart is heavy, low on power, on hard tyres at high pressure, with rear brakes only, so it is a momentum vehicle (Kart Class, Autosport forum). Everything below follows from that.

1. Look through the corner. At the apex while braking, at the exit at the apex, at the next corner on the exit (Red Bull, 7 mistakes novices make). Kart-Map calls a short gaze "the number one mistake affecting 90% of beginners". Terence Dove: fix eyes on the apex well before braking, switch to the exit before the apex.
2. Brake in a straight line, hard first, then ease off, never locked. Club100's Whilton bite-size guide for beginners: brake much earlier than the post to start with, "then every lap, brake a little bit later and a little bit harder". Dove's free braking chapter: aim for 97.9 per cent of the pressure that locks, and if the rears lock, release. Red Bull: hit hard so they almost lock, then release smoothly towards the apex.
3. Stop over-slowing. "Rental karts don't have the horsepower once a kart has slowed down too much" (Kart Class). CRG: mid-corner oversteer means you entered too hard, not that you should brake more.
4. Full throttle as early as it will take it, and hold it. Ross Bentley: "it isn't how early you touch the gas pedal, but how soon you can get to full throttle", and a throttle-then-lift costs about a tenth a corner. Kart Class: a dipping engine note means the throttle came late. Kart Directory on Whilton's 390s: respect the throttle on exit or the back steps out.
5. No sliding. A sliding tyre scrubs speed and "rarely works every lap" (Kartletix); "excessive sliding isn't the fastest way to drive with rental karts" (Kart Class).
6. Turn in late for a late apex and drive the exit. Kart-Map: a late apex is "almost always faster than an early apex"; if you run wide flat out, the apex was too early. Daytona: exit speed matters more than entry speed.
7. Hands at quarter to three, fixed, push the wheel, small inputs. Daytona: "99% of the time, drivers would be faster if they were smooth on all of the controls". Kart Class: hire karters are aggressive on the wheel because of the kart's weight.
8. Zero spins and even laps beat one hero lap. Flow Racers, TKART, Grassroots Motorsports and Daytona all put consistency first in an enduro.
9. The first two laps of a stint are learning laps: cold hard tyres, and every kart is different (Kart Class; BUKC driver guide; Club100 driver guide).
10. Let faster karts through on a straight, off the line, never in a braking zone, and copy their line and brake lights (Kart Class flag guide; CRG). One defensive move only, and the overtaker needs its nose alongside by the apex to be owed room (Motorsport UK karting guidelines, April 2025).

Not worth chasing: seat position and body lean are worth tenths at most, and sources contradict each other on lean (TeamSport says both sit square and lean out on different pages; Daytona says rest against the outside of the seat). Sit square, sit back from the wheel, arms slightly bent.

Trail braking: the sources split, and the N35 cuts the throttle on any brake touch, so for a novice the answer is finish the braking straight, off the brake at turn-in, no overlap.

## 3. Team craft for a hire enduro

- Driver order. Not the reckless fast driver and not the consistent qualifier at the start (TKART, K1 Speed and Flow Racers endurance guides); the calmest driver starts, the quickest and calmest finishes. Everyone drives one stint per heat.
- The change. Old driver out to the left, new driver in from the right; grippy gloves for the second half of a stint; pit signs and hand signals (endurance forum advice; Club100 bans pit-to-kart radio). Daytona: these races "are rarely won by the team with the fastest lap time" and "may be won or lost in this critical handover". Amateur car racers' heart rates spike to about 177 at the driver change against 141 for professionals (Barthel 2020), so rehearse it on the ground until it is calm. Target: twenty seconds stationary (a US arrive-and-drive series times changes at 20 s; no Whilton figure exists).
- Pit lane at walking pace. At the Daytona 24 hours the good teams have a captain walk the kart in so nobody collects a speeding penalty (Daytona live updates). Whilton's pit lane speed rule and penalty list were not found; the venue says sporting regulations are available on request.
- The start. Position towards the inside, expect contact when three karts jockey, "the start is only the beginning" (Kart Class). Rolling or standing was not found: ask.
- Spun or stalled. Stay in the kart, hands on the wheel, wait for the marshal; a spin is a yellow flag and a marshal restart. Roughly a lap lost (reasoned from 60 to 70 s laps, not sourced).
- Protection. Neck or back strain is the second most common go-kart injury and kart-to-kart collision the most common cause (Alam 2026, US series); two UK case reports describe scarves caught in the rear wheel (Brimioulle 2017, Ahmad 2011). No scarves, snoods or loose neck wear, hair tied, sit back from the wheel, a rib protector if you own one.
- Fitness. A 30-minute indoor kart race measured as moderate exertion (average heart rate about 133, lactate under 2 mmol/L, Sperlich 2014); forearm fatigue from steering is real after short runs (Zarebski 2025). Stints of 11 to 14 minutes need no training, only a relaxed grip on the straights.
- Hydration. In cool conditions 1 to 3 per cent dehydration did not change simulated lap times (Mollica 2019, Holland 2019). Drink to thirst through the day, a cup before each stint, nothing forced.
- Rain. The same three stops for everyone, so no strategy call; the calmest driver takes the wettest stint. The app's Wet switch turns every corner call into that corner's wet plan (docs/validation.md holds the UK Karting wet article and the CUAC note that the chicane run-off becomes a bog).
- Kart lottery. No coach has a technique for a slow kart; the only remedy in the literature is kart changes (Karting4u). Note the kart number in practice and ask the organiser whether karts are swapped at the stops.

## 4. The two weeks before

- The app's Start tab, in order: map, the corner cards, the walk test at home, the practice loop, then the coach. Ten minutes a day.
- Lap in your head, the way the imagery evidence says it works: seated as in the kart, hands up on an imagined wheel, eyes shut, first person, feel the brake and the wheel as well as see the corner, at real lap timing, ten to fifteen minutes, three times a week, straight after watching an onboard lap (PETTLEP; Schuster 2011 across 133 studies; Callow 2013 and 2017 cut simulator slalom lap times with internal visual plus kinaesthetic imagery). Imagery added to physical practice gives a small to medium gain (d 0.43, Simonsmeier 2020; g 0.58, Lindsay 2021); on its own it only maintains a skill (Post 2015).
- Watch the drivers who top the timing screens on the day, and copy their line and brake lights (Driver61; CRG). The rear LED brake lights on rental karts make braking points easy to see.
- Two minutes of debrief after every stint, one thing at a time (Kart-Map: "two minutes of debriefing after a session is worth ten extra sessions"). The app's debrief now opens with "where do you think you lost time?" before it tells you, because learners who estimate their own error first keep more of the correction (Liu 1997).

## 5. Race day checklist

1. Briefing: format, layout and chicane, start type, pit lane rule, engine on or off at the change, kart swaps, earpieces allowed, track limits at Pit Bend.
2. Set the app: layout, chicane, dry or wet, driver level. Each driver's name before Start GPS.
3. Practice session: two laps at eight tenths, three laps on one thing, the in-lap. The quickest driver last, for qualifying.
4. Rehearse the change on the ground, twice.
5. In the kart: one steering movement per corner, eyes on the exit, brake straight, throttle progressively, flat where the list says flat. Let faster karts through on a straight.
6. After each stint: the debrief's one change, said to the next driver in one sentence.
7. Save the recording; keep the team file.

## 6. What the coaching science says about how the app should talk

- Cue wording. External-focus cues ("brake at the post", "aim for the kerb") beat internal ones ("press the pedal") by Hedges g 0.26 for performance and 0.58 for retention across 73 studies (Chua 2021), but a 2024 reanalysis finds strong publication bias and corrected effects of g 0.01 to 0.15 (McKay 2024), and the one simulated-driving test found no lap-time effect (Harris 2018). Cost of adopting it: nil. The app's calls already name landmarks and outcomes; the new brake-pump tip was worded as an outcome ("one squeeze of the brake, not two").
- Feedback after every lap. The guidance hypothesis says frequent feedback hurts retention, but a 75-comparison meta-analysis finds no reliable effect of reduced frequency (McKay 2022). What does replicate: instant feedback learns worse than feedback a few seconds later (Swinnen 1990; Anderson 2001), and estimating your own error first improves retention (Liu 1997). So the app keeps one focus corner per lap, now speaks the coaching point about three seconds after the lap time, and asks the self-estimate question in the debrief.
- Prescriptive beats descriptive for novices: "do this" outperforms "you did that" (Oppici 2021). The app's tips are prescriptive.
- Spoken cues under load. Novices have about 250 ms less spare attention while driving (Patten 2006); a pre-alert 1 s before a warning helps, 300 ms before hurts (Wiese 2004); warning lead times of 2.5 s work but load the driver, 4.5 s or more is comfortable (Zhang 2019; Liu 2023). The app's chirp about a second before the brake word matches Wiese; whether a 1.6 s word lead plus the chirp is enough for a hire kart is untested and is the thing to measure on the practice loop.
- Tone or speech. Speech is preferred and gave faster hands-on responses in one takeover study (Forster 2017), while a tone beat speech on takeover quality in another (Chai 2024). The app offers both; race day should use words plus tones, talk-me-round only in practice.
- Errorless progression. Learners who start easy and make few errors build skills that survive pressure (Maxwell 2001; Masters 1992), evidence level 3 (Kal 2018). The novice level's "a wheel's width off the kerb first" follows this.
- Track familiarisation. No study of map or video pre-briefing in karting exists. Racing drivers reach their session asymptote within nine laps and the first lap predicts it (Franceschini 2017); video and simulator familiarisation improve first exposure in automated-driving studies, fading after three exposures (Ebnali 2019, 2020; Hergeth 2017). Expect the first two laps to be the learning laps, whatever the homework.

## 7. Open questions to settle before the day

- Heats and final, or a single enduro; the layout; rolling or standing start; kart swaps; pit lane speed and minimum stop; engine on or off at the change. Ask the organiser's office or compsec@whiltonmill.co.uk.
- Earpieces under the helmet and a phone in a suit pocket at a Whilton hire event: no written rule was found. The venue's briefing decides; the app works with tones only if speech is not allowed.
- Last year's result sheet: the seconds-per-lap and laps gap from last to mid-field is the real target and only the printout or the day's timing gives it.
- The N35 throttle cut: how far before turn-in a novice must be off the brake is a hand test in practice.
