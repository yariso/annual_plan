# Whilton Mill line guide and audio coach: project brief for Claude Code

## What this is
A single-file web app (dist/index.html) for a novice kart driver at Whilton Mill, UK. Five tabs, Start first and the default on load:
- Start: the journey in order. 1 Before the day (buttons into read me the lap, the virtual lap, the lap in your head and the quiz, the layout's corners by flat, lift and brake from `pedalLists`, the videos to watch); 2 Test it at home (the walk test: `walkStart` draws the phone's GPS trace live on a small map with the accuracy circle, counts distance while ignoring standing-still jitter, speaks the signal quality and every 25 m; and the sound test); 3 At the circuit (a big button to the GPS coach); 4 After each stint (the debrief). `data-go` buttons jump to the right place on the Coach tab.
- Map: real track shape, racing line coloured 1 to 5 by braking effort, kerb verdicts, brake markers. Switches (visible on every tab): layout (International 1054 m, National about 860 m), driver level (novice, intermediate, advanced, model), 2024 chicane on or off, dry or wet. The intermediate level is built in build_data.py as the mean of the novice's and the advanced driver's brake zones, the novice's lifts at 60% length and the novice's coasts; its card text is `doI` where a corner has one, otherwise the advanced plan plus a standing note (`doFor`); its headline level is the novice's where the two differ (`lvOf`); its Talk me round sentence is the novice's where the levels differ, else the advanced one (`callFor`); the demo kart's grip and pace sit between the two.
- Corners: every corner in lap order. Each card has a pedal plan (how hard, from where, for how long), brake marker, line, apex kerb, exit kerb, mistakes, wet notes, what the model does there (`modelNote`: the hire kart's entry speed, brake or lift point and slowest speed from data/model.json), and a zoomed close-up.
- Plan: a one-page plan at the top (Move up the table: before the day, on the day, after each stint, with the layout's corners listed by flat, lift and brake from lvOf), session plan, a kerb verdict table for every corner (use, with care, stay off, look first; stay off everywhere in the wet), kerb summary, wet notes, hire-kart notes, validation record and sources. The layout, driver, chicane and wet controls stay visible on this tab.
- Model driver level: the lap time model's fastest lap for the hire kart. Its braking levels, line, tones and words come from data/model.json (see below). It is the limit for a perfect driver, and the app says so.
- Coach: the race-day panel first (driver name, a big Start GPS button, status, lap readouts, test sound, lock buttons), then collapsed sections: read me the lap, practice at home (a talked virtual lap on the map, the lap in your head with a rhythm check against the demo lap, a corner quiz), the demo lap on the map, the debrief and team table, the settings, how it works at the circuit. While GPS runs a black pocket screen (`#pocket`, `pocketShow`) covers the page with the lap readouts and the last word; touches do nothing and its button must be held for about two seconds to stop; it can be hidden and shown again. Live GPS, map lock, tones and words, lap time with the gap to best spoken each lap, GPS ready, lost and back announcements, lap-by-lap coaching, debrief. Read me the lap: a pre-race walkthrough that speaks every corner in order with the pedal plan, brake marker and kerb verdict.

## House style (owner preference, keep to it)
- UK English. Never use em dashes anywhere (build.py counts them). Plain headings, plain prose, no marketing tone.
- Every driving claim should trace to a source or be flagged as reasoned. See docs/validation.md.
- Human validation matters: never present estimates as measured.

## Layout of the repo
- src/app.src.html: the whole app (HTML, CSS, JS). `__DATA__` is replaced at build time.
- build/build_data.py: geometry to data. Reads data/*_px.npy (centrelines in image pixels) and data/scale.json, detects corners by curvature, names them per layout, builds the racing line (lateral offsets), dry and wet braking levels, kerbs, markers. Writes data/data.json.
- build/build.py: injects data.json into the source, writes dist/index.html.
- model/laptime.py: minimum lap time model. Track from data/data.json at 1 m with a smooth height profile through per-corner heights (HEIGHT_KEYS, a fraction of the hill parameter); a point-mass kart with a friction ellipse, engine power, drag, rolling resistance, rear-only brakes and gravity on the slope (KARTS: hire and race); a quasi-steady-state forward and backward pass (qss, numba) for the speed profile of any line; the line as a periodic spline of lateral offsets at 8 m control points bounded by the usable width; L-BFGS-B minimisation restarted from the shaken best line until five rounds bring no gain. Sweeps the hill, grip, width and the 1054 m lap length, and calibrates against the 56.99 s hire-kart record. Writes data/model.json (laps, per-corner speeds and brake points, braking levels at 2 m, line offsets and speeds, the round-by-round history) and docs/laptime-model.md via model/report.py. build/build.py embeds a trimmed copy as `__MODEL__`. `--quick` for a smoke test, `--retidy` to re-clean the level strings and rewrite the report without re-running.
- tests/test_coach.py: Playwright tests that run simulated laps through the real engine: words and tones, coaching on a deliberately poor driver, wet mode, and the map lock on rotated noisy synthetic GPS. Playwright's Chromium revision must match the browser installed (in the Claude Code web sandbox that is Playwright 1.56 with the preinstalled Chromium; do not run playwright install there).
- tools/: how the centrelines were traced from a map image (reference only).
- Build and test: `python build/build_data.py && python build/build.py && python tests/test_coach.py` (needs numpy, scipy, playwright with chromium).

## Engine design (src/app.src.html, second half of the script)
- Map lock (`fitOne`, `fitRecording`): ICP with a 2D similarity transform fits the map centreline to a GPS recording, tries both layouts, reports RMS and coverage. Stored in localStorage as `wm.lock`.
- Lap engine (`lapFix`, `lapTick`): each fix is transformed into map space and matched to the centreline (windowed nearest point). Position is carried forward between fixes (`advance`) along the driver's own speed profile scaled to today's pace: at each point the quickest of the last three laps (`lapProfile` at each lap end, `lapE.profs`), so a lap held up in traffic cannot slow the next lap's look-ahead; constant acceleration before the first lap. A measured fix age (`lapE.lagKnown`, from the position timestamp) replaces the gpsDelay allowance rather than adding to it; the fix age is logged as the recording's seventh column. Lap timing by start-line crossing.
- Audio: a continuous tone sounds for as long as the painted level is above 1 at the look-ahead position. Pitch encodes level (2 lift hum, 3 to 5 brake). Words (Hard brake, Firm brake, Dab, Lift, Flat) fire at a longer look-ahead; the word look-ahead never runs backwards, so a noisy fix cannot repeat a word. A rising chirp (`set.prepCue`) comes a second before each brake word. The word lead grows by the measured speech start (`speechLag`, median of `u.onstart` delays, capped at 1 s) when `set.autoLead` is on. `calibrateLead` learns a per-corner extra lead (`lapE.leadAdj`, 0 to 20 m) when the driver's measured brake onset lands later than the plan on three laps running, with a slack of a full fix spacing, so it only acts at 10 Hz and up; it moves that corner's word and chirp earlier, not the tone. The go blip (back to flat) waits two ticks of level 1 so a backward GPS snap cannot play it inside a brake zone. Talk-me-round mode speaks a sentence per corner. After three seconds without a fix the tone stops rather than extrapolating; live mode says GPS lost after four seconds and GPS back when fixes resume. Lap times are spoken with the gap to best ("71.9, 0.4 off", "71.4, best", "equal best").
- Reader (`lapScript`, `readStart`): the pre-race walkthrough, built from CARDS (the level's text via `doFor`, or the wet text), the marker and the kerb verdicts (`kerbsOf`, `kerbSay`), spoken one corner at a time with a short pause between.
- Coaching (`lapMetrics`, `coachTips`): per-corner windows, entry speed, slowest speed, brake onset, throttle pick-up, segment time. Rules compare with the guide (flat, lift, brake) and with the driver's own best through that corner. Thresholds widen at low GPS rates. `finishLap` keeps one focus corner: the one costing most against the reference (the driver's best at 4 Hz and up; below that the median of the earlier laps once there are three, because the best at 1 Hz is the luckiest reading) where the loss comes with a rule tip or an apex slower than the best on two laps running; it is repeated each lap until the corner improves by the threshold (held for two laps at low rates), then confirmed ("Boot better, two tenths found"), and dropped after three or four laps without progress. What is spoken is the short form (`SHORT`: "Christmas, brake later."); the long form with the numbers goes to the debrief (`lap.tip` against `lap.say`). Next lap the focus corner's pedal word carries the cue once ("Hard brake, brake later"). A steady driver at 1 Hz is never coached on corner-time noise (tested). `cornerWindow` gives the stretch each corner is judged over and `modelSeg` times the model's hire-kart lap through the same stretch, so the focus tip can quote the driver's apex speed against their best and the model's. `sessionTable` in the debrief lists every lap by every corner with the model row and, after four laps, the spread and the least consistent corner.
- Practice at home (`virtualStart`, `refTimes`, `mindTap`, `mindReport`, `quizAsk`): the virtual lap is the demo in Talk me round mode with the mode restored on stop; `refTimes` runs the demo kart silently for a run-up lap and a recorded lap with the lap engine detached (lapE.T null, no fixes or ticks) to get the time at each corner; the lap in your head times the driver's taps against those and names the two stretches furthest out; the quiz asks corner order and pedal level from cardList and lvOf.
- Team table (`teamLog`, `teamTable`): every live GPS lap is kept on the phone (localStorage `wm.team`, capped at 300) tagged with the driver name typed above Start GPS; the debrief shows each driver's best lap and best time through each corner for the current layout, fastest of the team marked, with a clear button. Demo and replayed laps are not logged.
- Demo (`simStep`): a simulated kart follows the painted levels; "makes mistakes" brakes early, over-slows and lifts at flat corners so the coach has something to say. Emulated GPS (1, 10 or 25 Hz, noise, latency) feeds the same engine as live GPS.

## Known limits (be honest about these in any UI text)
- Phone browser GPS is about 1 fix per second. Tone start is good to roughly 10 m, tone end less. The brake onset the coach measures is only known to about half a fix spacing, which is why the learned lead needs three consistent laps and a segment time alone never picks a focus corner at that rate. A 10 to 25 Hz receiver removes the receiver as the limit; the traced map and the lock (a few metres) then set the floor.
- Geometry was traced from the Kart Directory circuit map image (a commercial map), checked against the owner's copy of the map (tools/check_map.py: 0.5 m RMS), and scaled so the International lap with the chicane measures 1054 m, the length in the 2025 club and BKC regulations. The venue advertises 1200 m and 960 m; the lap times (race karts about 45 s, hire karts 57 to 65 s) rule those out, see docs/validation.md. Corner detection runs on the earlier 1200 m scale (data/scale.json detect_scale) and the segments are mapped across. The lock's fitted scale reports the length measured from the trace. The marker positions (22, 24 and 14 m before turn-in) and the chicane approach length are assumed, not traced. For anything public, replace the trace with your own GPS survey. The map lock plus a clean recording can generate that.
- The lock fixes the layout only. The chicane switch, driver level and wet setting are taken from the Map tab and cannot be changed while GPS runs.
- The demo kart's top speed and grip are guesses, not measured; calibrate from recorded laps (backlog 3).
- The lap time model is built on estimates: the hill (8 m, from words in the guides), the track width (8 m, the usual kart-circuit width) and the kart's grip, traction and braking. A point mass has no yaw, no load transfer and no kerbs. docs/laptime-model.md compares it with real laps (Senior X30 45.28 s and a quick hire driver 64.54 s on the current layout, hire records 56 to 57 s and a Rotax Max 44.8 s before the chicane). Trust its relative results more than its lap time.
- Phone behaviour (GPS stopping when the phone locks, wake lock support, the silent switch, Bluetooth latency, speech needing a first utterance inside a tap) is from memory and needs a hand test on an iPhone and an Android phone.
- Braking levels 1 to 5, zone lengths and lateral line positions are a reading of the written guides, not measured data.
- The 2024 chicane has no written driving guide (researched September 2026, about 270 searches: docs/validation.md). Its card rests on the designer's line (stay right after The Boot, the chicane blends into Pit Bend), the shape (a tight left on new tarmac), the model's numbers, and hire-race facts (a bog beyond the braking zone, the N35's brake cutting the throttle, kerbs that launched karts on day one). No braking reference or apex has been published; the technique is in videos that could not be read. Its kerbs and the new Pit Bend kerb are undescribed, so the app says stay off or look first.
- Every corner-card sentence was checked against the written guides in September 2026 (docs/validation.md, Card check): 193 supported by quotes, 141 reasoned, 9 contradicted and fixed. Tarporley Karting Team's page is unreliable for corner order.
- Kerb verdicts come from the written guides where one covers the kerb (see docs/validation.md and the Plan tab table); the rest are marked Look first. Christmas, the Chapmans apex, the left of The Boot, the chicane, the Pit Bend apex and Zulu 1 and 2 have no source.
- Race-day content (flags, kit, briefing, formats, age and height limits) is from the venue's pages as seen in search excerpts and from general UK karting guides, not from the venue's briefing.
- Wet line is general technique except at The Boot, where UK Karting's wet-driving article gives a Whilton-specific instruction (on the card). Other Whilton-specific wet guides exist only as videos that could not be read.
- YouTube transcripts could not be fetched. docs/validation.md lists the videos to review by hand.
