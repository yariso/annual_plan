# Pass rates at Chilwell and nationally

Research compiled 20 September 2026.

## Method note, read this first

This is a serious limitation and the app author needs to know about it before using anything below.

Every gov.uk address is blocked by this session's network egress policy. Attempts to open
`www.gov.uk` and `assets.publishing.service.gov.uk` were refused by the proxy with a 403 policy
denial. I could not open, download or read the DVSA spreadsheets themselves. I could not open
`passrates.uk`, `thedrivinginstructordirectory.co.uk`, `newdriverprogramme.com` or any of the other
driving sites either, for the same reason. Direct page fetching was available only for two
mirrored copies of old gov.uk pages hosted on Amazon S3, and for github.com.

So: almost everything below comes from web search result snippets and the search engine's
summaries of those snippets, not from pages I opened and read. I have said, for each figure, which
site it is attributed to. Where sites disagree I have shown the disagreement rather than picking a
winner. Nothing here should be printed in the app as "official DVSA figure" without somebody
opening the actual spreadsheet at the gov.uk links given in the Sources section and checking it.

Treat the national figures as reasonably well corroborated (several independent sites agree, and
the numbers are internally consistent). Treat the Chilwell centre figures as indicative, because
the sites disagree by up to 6 percentage points.

## Summary

1. The national car practical pass rate for 2024-25 (April 2024 to March 2025) was 48.7 per cent,
   from 1,836,558 tests, of which 893,609 passed and 942,949 failed. Multiple sites agree and the
   arithmetic checks out. [passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)
2. The national rate for 2025-26 (April 2025 to March 2026) rose to about 50.0 to 50.1 per cent,
   from roughly 1,990,665 car practical tests. [passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-statistics-uk-2026)
3. The rate has kept climbing into 2026. August 2026 was 52.1 per cent, described as the highest
   monthly figure since April 2021, and the rate has been above 50 per cent for nine consecutive
   months. [driving.org](https://www.driving.org/dvsa-delivers-record-2-13-million-driving-tests-and-claims-waiting-times-are-falling-1/)
4. Nottingham (Chilwell) was well below average in 2024-25. Reported figures for that year cluster
   at 43.9 to 44.2 per cent, on roughly 6,213 tests. [drivingtestroutes.com](https://www.drivingtestroutes.com/driving-test-routes-in-nottingham-chilwell/), [passdrivingtest.co.uk](https://passdrivingtest.co.uk/nottingham-chilwell-driving-test-centre/NThiLSpF)
5. Chilwell improved sharply in 2025-26, to 49.89 per cent on 9,415 tests. Two separate sites give
   essentially the same number. [thedrivinginstructordirectory.co.uk](https://www.thedrivinginstructordirectory.co.uk/driving-test-centres/nottingham-chilwell), [bookdrivingtest.uk](https://bookdrivingtest.uk/test-centres/)
6. That improvement, if real, roughly closes the gap to the national average. Chilwell at 49.89 per
   cent against a national 50.0 to 50.1 per cent is broadly average, not "hard". (reasoned)
7. The first attempt pass rate at Chilwell in 2025-26 was 49.22 per cent across 4,888 first
   attempts, and 126 of those first-timers passed with zero driving faults. [thedrivinginstructordirectory.co.uk](https://www.thedrivinginstructordirectory.co.uk/driving-test-centres/nottingham-chilwell)
8. Chilwell's best recent month was January 2026 at 56.41 per cent, from 453 passes out of 803
   tests. [thedrivinginstructordirectory.co.uk](https://www.thedrivinginstructordirectory.co.uk/driving-test-centres/nottingham-chilwell)
9. Chilwell's test volume grew by roughly half between 2024-25 and 2025-26, from about 6,213 to
   9,415 tests, which matches DVSA's national push to record test numbers. (reasoned, from the two
   volume figures cited above)
10. Nearby Nottinghamshire centres generally do better than Chilwell did in 2024-25. Nottingham
    (Colwick) around 47 per cent, Sutton-in-Ashfield around 51.2 per cent, Watnall around 48.9 to
    52.9 per cent. [drivingtestroutes.com](https://drivingtestroutes.com/nottingham-colwick-driving-test-centre/), [drivingtestroutes.com](https://drivingtestroutes.com/driving-test-routes-in-ashfield/), [passdrivingtest.co.uk](https://passdrivingtest.co.uk/watnall-driving-test-centre/pzIxWsxg)
11. Beware the phrase "national average" on driving sites. Some quote 48.7 per cent (the real
    test-weighted national rate) and some quote 50.2 or 52.2 per cent (the unweighted mean of
    individual centre rates, which is higher because tiny rural centres count the same as
    Wolverhampton). [audrive.net](https://audrive.net/en/uk/pass-rates)
12. The table the task brief named, DRT0201, is a national table, not a centre table. It is
    "Practical car test pass rates by gender, monthly: Great Britain". The centre level table is
    DRT122A. (see Findings, Where the data actually lives)
13. DVSA table references changed in January 2025 when DVSA took over publication from DfT. Old
    centre-level reference DVSA0201 became DRT122A. [passrates.uk methodology](https://passrates.uk/methodology)
14. On gender, the best supported current figure is men 49.5 per cent and women 47.6 per cent for
    2024-25, a gap of 1.9 percentage points. [passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)
15. A widely repeated "men 51 per cent, women 47 per cent" also circulates. And a "male 43.1 per
    cent, female 47.2 per cent" pair circulates which is almost certainly the theory test, where
    women do better, not the practical. Do not mix these up. (reasoned)
16. No male and female split for Chilwell specifically was found, although DVSA publishes one in
    DRT122A. It would need the spreadsheet opening.
17. DVSA does not appear to publish a headline "average number of attempts" figure. What it
    publishes is pass rate by attempt number. About 53 per cent of tests are first attempts and 25
    per cent are second attempts. [passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-second-attempt-pass-rate)
18. Pass rates barely fall for the second, third and fourth attempts. They only drop away from the
    fifth on. Second attempt about 49.5 per cent, fifth 44.3 per cent, sixth or later 38.8 per
    cent. [passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-second-attempt-pass-rate)
19. Roughly nine in ten people who keep taking the test pass within three attempts.
    [passmefast.co.uk](https://www.passmefast.co.uk/resources/get-test-ready/practical-test/average-attempts-driving-test)
20. Useful for reassurance in the app: needing a second go is the normal experience, not a failure
    of ability, and your odds on attempt two are about the same as on attempt one. (reasoned, from
    the attempt data above)

### National pass rate, 2024-25

The headline year most sites are still quoting is 2024-25, meaning April 2024 to March 2025.

Pass rate 48.7 per cent, more precisely 48.65 per cent.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

Tests conducted 1,836,558 category B (car) practical tests.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

Passes 893,609. Fails 942,949.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

Number of centres in the count: 327 DVSA centres.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

Context given: this was the highest annual rate since 2017-18, when it was 47.4 per cent, and 0.7
percentage points above the 2023-24 rate of 48.0 per cent.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

First time pass rate nationally in 2024-25: 48.9 per cent, marginally above the overall 48.7 per
cent. [passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

Auto Express independently describes the 48.7 per cent figure and says it comes from combing

### National pass rate, 2025-26 and into 2026

Pass rate for the year to March 2026: reported as 50.0 per cent by one source and 50.1 per cent by

### The "national average" trap

Different sites quote very different national averages, and the app will look wrong if it copies
one without understanding it.

48.7 per cent is the test-weighted national rate for 2024-25, that is, total passes divided by
total tests across Great Britain. This is the real national pass rate.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

52.2 per cent is the unweighted average of individual centre pass rates across 321 centres. One
site states this explicitly: "this is an average of centres, not a national pass rate, with each
centre's published rate averaged together without weighting by how many tests they actually run, so
a small regional centre counts the same as a busy metropolitan one."
[audrive.net](https://audrive.net/en/uk/pass-rates)

50.2 per cent is used as a "national average" by at least one site alongside a 2024/25 centre
figure. Given the two numbers above, 50.2 per cent is most likely another unweighted centre
average, or a 2025-26 national figure being compared against a 2024-25 centre figure, which would
be an error on that site's part. Either way it is not comparable with 48.7 per cent.
[drivingtestroutes.com](https://www.drivingtestroutes.com/driving-test-routes-in-nottingham-chilwell/) (the explanation is reasoned)

Practical consequence: Chilwell at 44.2 per cent is 4.5 points below the real national average of
48.7 per cent for 2024-25, not 6.0 points below as one site claims. The app should use the 48.7 per
cent comparator. (reasoned)

Chilwell fits this pattern. It is a suburban centre serving a built-up area with tram crossings,
dual carriageways and busy retail roads, so a pass rate a little below the national average is what
you would expect. (reasoned)

### Male and female split

National, 2024-25: men 49.5 per cent, women 47.6 per cent, a gap of 1.9 percentage points,
described as narrow and fairly steady across recent years.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

A competing figure in circulation: men 51 per cent, women 47 per cent, described as a roughly 4
point gap consistent over multiple decades. This conflicts with the 1.9 point figure above.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-statistics-uk-2026) (the attribution is
uncertain because it came through a search summary, so treat as unverified)

A third pair in circulation: male 43.1 per cent, female 47.2 per cent. This has women ahead, which
is the opposite of the practical test pattern, and 43.1 and 47.2 match the reported theory test
figures below almost exactly. This pair is almost certainly the theory test, not the practical.
(reasoned)

Theory test, April 2025 to March 2026: overall 44.8 per cent, with women at 47.2 per cent and men
at 43.0 per cent. [drivingtheorytest.io](https://drivingtheorytest.io/pass-rate/)

So the pattern to state in the app, carefully: on the practical test men pass at a slightly higher
rate than women. On the theory test women pass at a higher rate than men. Both gaps are small.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025), [drivingtheorytest.io](https://drivingtheorytest.io/pass-rate/)

The practical test gap narrows with attempts. The gap is largest on first attempts, shrinks by the
second attempt, and has almost disappeared by the third.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

Reported explanation: the gap is structural, relating to lesson hours, private practice rates and
anxiety reporting, rather than driving ability, and the long-run pass rate after retakes is similar
across genders. [passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

DVSA commissioned TRL to study this. The report is PPR2063, "Equality in the driving test". One
reported finding is that the performance of male candidates is less affected by their examiner.
[trl.co.uk](https://www.trl.co.uk/publications/equality-in-the-driving-test), PDF at [trl.co.uk](https://www.trl.co.uk/uploads/trl/documents/PPR2063-Equality-in-the-driving-test.pdf)
(I could not open the PDF, so the finding is unverified)

Gender split for Chilwell specifically: not found. DVSA does publish it, in DRT122A, which gives
per-centre per-month conducted and passed counts split male, female and total.
[passrates.uk methodology](https://passrates.uk/methodology)

Recommendation for the app: given the conflict between the 1.9 point and 4 point figures, and given
this app is for one named person, the honest thing is to mention the gender gap only briefly, say
it is small, and say the difference disappears with practice and retakes. Do not build a feature
around it. (reasoned)

### Number of attempts

There does not appear to be an official DVSA headline statistic for "the average number of attempts
to pass". What DVSA publishes is the pass rate broken down by which attempt the candidate is on.
One source states directly that there has not been a study large enough to produce a reliable
national average for the exact number of attempts needed.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/first-time-pass-rate-explained)

Distribution of tests by attempt number: 53 per cent of practical tests are taken by candidates on
their first attempt, and 25 per cent by candidates on their second attempt.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-second-attempt-pass-rate)

That leaves about 22 per cent of all tests being third or later attempts. (reasoned)

Pass rate by attempt, national, 2024-25:

First attempt 48.9 per cent.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-2024-2025)

Second attempt around 49.5 per cent, marginally higher than the first.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-second-attempt-pass-rate)

A competing figure of 50.4 per cent for the second attempt also circulates.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/first-time-pass-rate-explained) (unverified, the two
figures come from the same site via different search summaries)

Third attempt around 49.1 per cent, broadly the same as the first attempt baseline. A competing
figure of 48.7 per cent also circulates.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-second-attempt-pass-rate)

Third and fourth attempt rates stay close to the second. The rate only falls away noticeably from
the fifth attempt onwards.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-second-attempt-pass-rate)

Fifth attempt 44.3 per cent.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-second-attempt-pass-rate)

Sixth attempt or beyond, grouped together, 38.8 per cent.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-second-attempt-pass-rate)

Roughly nine in ten people who keep taking the test pass within three attempts.
[passmefast.co.uk](https://www.passmefast.co.uk/resources/get-test-ready/practical-test/average-attempts-driving-test), [passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/first-time-pass-rate-explained)

This is the single most useful set of numbers in this research for the app's tone. The honest
message is: about half of all tests end in a pass, a bit under half of first-timers pass, and if
you fail your odds on the next attempt are essentially unchanged. Failing once tells you very
little about whether you will pass next time. (reasoned)

### Pass rate by age, for context

First time pass rate is strongly age related. Reported figures: 17 to 18 year olds around 61 per
cent, and candidates aged 45 and over around 36 per cent.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/driving-test-pass-rate-by-age)

A 17 year old first time pass rate of 60.75 per cent is reported, against around 45 per cent for
the 30 plus cohort, a gap of 15.75 percentage points on first attempt, described as sharper than
the gap on the overall rate.
[passrates.uk](https://passrates.uk/guide/first-time-driving-test-pass-rate-by-age)

This matters for how the app frames the odds. If the named user is an older learner, the relevant
comparator is not 48.7 per cent. Use this gently and only if the app knows the user's age.
(reasoned)

### Preparation hours

A 2014 DVSA study is widely quoted as finding that the average learner needs 42.6 hours of
professional instruction plus 15.7 hours of practice with friends and family before being test
ready. [passmefast.co.uk](https://www.passmefast.co.uk/resources/get-test-ready/practical-test/average-attempts-driving-test)

This figure is from 2014 and I could not find a more recent DVSA replacement for it. Label it with
its date if the app uses it. (reasoned)

DVSA runs a "Ready to Pass?" campaign aimed at learners taking the test only when genuinely ready.
The campaign site was updated in July 2026 to focus on 27 driving skills and on readiness for safe
independent driving rather than on passing the test.
[despatch.blog.gov.uk](https://despatch.blog.gov.uk/2026/07/09/driving-skills-whats-changed-on-the-ready-to-pass-website-and-why/)

A DVSA learner survey reportedly found a pass rate of 61.7 per cent for learners who said they did
not take private practice and 63.5 per cent for learners who said they did. These numbers are far
above the real national pass rate, so they must come from a self-selected survey subset rather than
from test records. Do not present them as pass rates.
[despatch.blog.gov.uk](https://despatch.blog.gov.uk/) (unverified, and the specific post URL was
not confirmed)

### Where the data actually lives

This section matters because the task brief named DRT0201, and DRT0201 is not the right table.

DRT0201 is "Practical car test pass rates by gender, monthly: Great Britain". It is a national
table. It has no test centre breakdown.
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/165/1658944.html)

DRT0202 is "Practical car test pass rates by number of attempts, age and gender: Great Britain".
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/165/1658944.html)

DRT0203 is "Practical car test pass rates by gender and age of candidate: Great Britain".
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/165/1658944.html)

DRT0101 is "Practical driving and riding test pass rates by test class, quarterly: Great Britain"
and DRT0102 is the theory equivalent.
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/165/1658944.html)

The test centre level tables were historically published under DVSA references, not DRT ones:

[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/169/1692795.html)

DVSA1204, "Car pass rates by ethnicity of candidate, year and test centre", ODS, yearly since 2007.
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/169/1692795.html)

DVSA1205, "Car driving test cancellation reasons by test centre", ODS, monthly since April 2007.
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/169/1692795.html)

DVSA1206, "Automatic car pass rates by gender and test centre", ODS, yearly since 2007. Relevant if
the user is taking an automatic test.
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/169/1692795.html)

DVSA0221, "Car and trailer pass rates by gender and test centre", ODS, yearly since 2007.
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/169/1692795.html)

In January 2025 DVSA took over responsibility for publishing all driving test, theory test and
driving instructor data from DfT, and the table references changed. The centre level car practical
table is now DRT122A, "car practical pass rates by gender, month and DTC", giving per-centre
per-month conducted and passed counts split male, female and total. The related tables run DRT122A
through DRT122E. [passrates.uk methodology](https://passrates.uk/methodology)

The reference numbering scheme was explained as: first digit 1 for car, second digit 2 for
practical, third digit 2 for test centre level.
[passrates.uk methodology](https://passrates.uk/methodology)

The data is published under the Open Government Licence v3.0, so the app can reproduce the figures
provided it attributes them. [passrates.uk methodology](https://passrates.uk/methodology)

Historically the gov.uk page for test centre data carried a warning that this data "is not classed
as an 'official statistic'" and is therefore "not subject to scrutiny and assessment by the UK
Statistics Authority". Worth checking whether that caveat still applies to the DRT122 series.
[gov.uk mirror on S3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/169/1692795.html)
